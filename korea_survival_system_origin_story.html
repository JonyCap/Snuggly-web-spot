Dong-A University · Busan, Korea
The starting point
It started with being lost — literally.
Living as an international student in Korea is exciting, but the first months are often disorienting. Not because Korea is unwelcoming — it isn't — but because the information students need is scattered, incomplete, or simply in a language they cannot yet read.
Years of personal experience living and studying in Korea
One gap no structured survival guide existed for incoming exchange students
The problem
What exchange students actually go through
These aren't hypothetical struggles. Every one of these was observed — or personally experienced — during years of living in Busan.
Information overload
Too much online, none of it organized or reliable for newcomers
Language barrier
Korean systems — transit, banking, apps — are often not in English
Asking strangers
Students rely on random people who give partial or incorrect answers
Missing the right apps
Essential Korean apps for daily life go completely unknown for weeks
Cultural confusion
Unspoken social norms lead to awkward or uncomfortable situations
Months of adjustment
What could take days often takes months without proper guidance
"I remembered thinking — if only someone had given me a clear, honest guide during my first week, everything would have been so much easier. Not a brochure. A real system."
— Me, reflecting on early days in Busan
The journey
From personal confusion to a clear idea
The idea didn't arrive in one moment. I built slowly — through experiences, observations, and conversations with other students who faced the same walls.
Phase 01
Arriving and struggling
The first weeks in Korea felt overwhelming. Simple tasks — registering a phone, finding the right bus, understanding university admin systems — were far harder than expected. Every small obstacle cost hours of searching, asking, and guessing.
Phase 02
Noticing the pattern
Over time, it became clear this wasn't a personal problem — it was universal. Other international students at Dong-A faced the same walls. New students asked the same questions semester after semester, often getting different or incomplete answers each time.
Phase 03
Building personal knowledge
After years of living in Korea, the knowledge accumulated naturally — which apps actually matter, how Korean etiquette works in practice, how to navigate the transport system confidently, where to find good food without guessing. This knowledge was real, tested, and useful. But it only existed informally.
Phase 04
The idea crystallizes
The question became simple: what if all of this — practical survival tips, cultural understanding, transportation guides, app recommendations, etiquette — existed in one place, designed specifically for incoming exchange students at Dong-A? Not a Wikipedia page. Not a forum thread. A real, structured, interactive system.
The solution
What the Korea Survival System covers
It was designed not as a website, but as a complete support system — giving future students a head start before they even land in Busan.
